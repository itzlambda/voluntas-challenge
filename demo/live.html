Monotonic time from receiving a WebSocket frame to finishing the SBE encoding of each emitted price-level record. Only the publishing copy counts. Encoding and latency recording continue even when no stream clients are subscribed. Snapshots, source switches and later file or socket writes are excluded. A message with 50 changed levels gives 50 observations.

p50 and p99 are histogram estimates over five-minute windows. Bucket bounds near the median are 10, 25, 50 and 100 µs, then 250 µs, 500 µs, 1 ms and 2.5 ms, so small differences are not meaningful. A percentile above the largest bucket shows as more than 60 s.

When no updates arrive, there are no new latency observations. The five-minute window can keep earlier samples visible; idle time does not count as zero latency. A failed scrape is shown as a gap.

The chart uses a logarithmic scale.

Prometheus scrapes the collector every second. A point is drawn only if the scrape succeeded and the collector published within the previous 15 seconds. Missing periods are left blank, never drawn as zero. Bars show the increase per interval; intervals without a successful scrape are blank. Counters restart from zero when the collector restarts.

Metrics: collector_receive_to_encode_seconds, collector_events_total.

Without a current book is desired minus synchronized markets. A market is synchronized when its publishing copy has both outcome books on its current connection. Without a ready standby is desired minus markets whose other copy is also ready. A ready standby requires a publishing copy and a second copy with both outcome books synchronized, so markets without a current book count in both lines. These counts do not prove that every book is still receiving updates, or that discovery found every market on the exchange.

Polymarket sometimes lists markets as active before their order books are available, which may explain small persistent gaps.

Metrics: collector_worker_busy_seconds_total.

Share of wall time the single output task spends on records, excluding idle waits; it is not CPU use. In file mode the writer converts and encodes records and writes or flushes output, including snapshots and chunk rotation. In stream mode the stream hub encodes every output record and routes its bytes to subscribers; bytes with no subscriber are discarded after encoding. Sustained values near 100% indicate little writer headroom; compare with waiting on the writer and internal latency.

Values use a one-minute rate. Missing or stale periods are left blank. Counters restart from zero when the collector restarts.

Metrics: collector_writer_busy_seconds_total.

Combined time all book workers wait after the output channel reports full, averaged over one minute. Output goes to the file writer or the stream hub. The value is milliseconds of worker wait per second, not time per event. Multiple workers can wait together, so the value can exceed 1,000.

Metrics: collector_book_mismatches_total, collector_older_full_books_skipped_total, collector_decode_errors_total.

Decoded market-data events per second on each upstream order-book socket, primary and replica, over a one-minute rate. Full books count, so a reconnect's burst of full books raises the rate for about a minute. Heartbeats and control messages do not count. There are no per-market rates.

The distribution includes connected sockets with markets and a rate. Exactly zero has its own bar. Sockets that are disconnected, have no markets, are warming up or have no current rate are counted separately and never shown as zero.

Bins are fitted to the rates when the page loads and stay fixed between refreshes so bars stay comparable. Rates far outside the fitted range go to the end bars. Use Fit range to refit.

Socket telemetry is scraped every 15 seconds. A rate needs four samples, so it appears about a minute after a new socket task starts or the collector restarts. Reconnects keep the counter, and is shown only while the socket keeps publishing fresh telemetry. History loads for the selected socket only; gaps mean no rate, and dashed lines mark collector restarts, after which the same ID may carry different markets.
